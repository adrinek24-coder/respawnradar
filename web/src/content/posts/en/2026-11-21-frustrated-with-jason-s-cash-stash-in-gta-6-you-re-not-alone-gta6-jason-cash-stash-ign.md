## Quick Answer (TL;DR)

Following Grand Theft Auto VI's November 19, 2026 launch on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, Jason's cash stash has quickly become one of the game's most talked-about early-game quirks. The UI for selecting how much money to deposit or withdraw—shared by both Jason's floorboard stash and ATMs—counts by a single dollar per tap but skyrockets into the thousands the moment you hold the D-pad. Even though the slider is frustrating, do not skip it: until you unlock the BuckMe banking app, Jason's bedroom floorboards are your best way to avoid losing all your cash when you get Wasted or Busted.

## Why Players Are Frustrated with Jason's Stash

You gain access to Jason's floorboard stash within the first hour of GTA 6, making this UI speed bump impossible to miss. Across IGN, Reddit, and ResetEra, players have highlighted the same pain points:

- **No quick-step increments:** Tapping the D-pad adjusts your transaction by $1 at a time, which is far too slow for large payouts.
- **Runaway acceleration:** Holding the D-pad ramps up so aggressively that players joke it jumps "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10,000".
- **No keypad input:** Even though other parts of GTA 6 feature interactive keypads, neither Jason's stash nor ATMs currently let you type an exact figure or click +100/-100 presets.
- **Costly rage-quits:** Players who gave up on the UI and carried their entire bankroll around Leonida learned the hard way that dying once wipes out pocket cash.

## How to Find and Use Jason's Floorboard Stash

- **Unlock the safehouse:** Jason Duval's house in Key Lento, Mariana County opens at the end of the mission *Gone Fishing*.
- **Head to the bedroom:** Walk up the outside stairs into the house and enter Jason's bedroom.
- **Locate the loose planks:** Stand near the foot of the bed and look toward the floor until the stash interaction prompt appears.
- **Open the compartment:** Hold **Triangle** on PS5 (or **Y** on Xbox Series X|S) to pull up the loose floorboards.
- **Manage cash or keepsakes:** Choose **CASH** to deposit or withdraw bills, or select **ITEMS** to inspect Jason's personal keepsakes and photos.
- **Track your balances:** Inside the stash menu, the house icon shows stored floor cash. While exploring, press **D-pad Down** to view your wallet cash and BuckMe balance in the top-right corner.

## Pro Tips and Crucial Chapter 1 Warning

- **Beat the finicky slider:** Instead of fighting the acceleration to leave an exact amount like $250 in your wallet, hold the D-pad briefly to dump your entire wallet in one go, then withdraw only a quick rough buffer when you actually need pocket money.
- **Always check the roadside mailbox:** Story payouts from Brian, Cal, and Donnie are dropped in the mailbox outside Jason's house and go straight into your wallet—not the bedroom stash—so bank or hide them right away.
- **Upgrade your guns early:** Pull cash from the floorboards early in Chapter 1 to buy weapon attachments like a suppressor from Ammu-Nation in Key Lento, which makes early firefights far cleaner.
- **Empty the stash before ending Chapter 1 (spoiler-light):** Do not treat Jason's floorboards as permanent storage. Before triggering the late Chapter 1 point-of-no-return mission *Tourist Trap*, withdraw everything from Jason's stash because you lose the house and any money left inside once the chapter wraps up. Because early guides report differing outcomes for checking balances at the chapter transition, transferring spare money into **Family Savings** via BuckMe—or spending it on permanent gear like weapons, attachments, and clothes—is the safest play.
